## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
Evidence supports **exercise interventions** (improving motor skills) and **behavioral therapies** (e.g., naturalistic developmental interventions) for autism in children, alongside **dietary approaches** (e.g., modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation) that may reduce core symptom severity. These findings are limited to specific outcomes (motor skills, behavioral symptoms) and do not address broader developmental or social communication domains.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills. [1]

**Immediate context**
Direct evidence supports **motor skill enhancement via exercise** and **short-term behavioral symptom reduction with modified ketogenic diet + rehabilitation**, but broader developmental or social outcomes remain understudied. No evidence addresses core autism symptom management beyond behavioral scales (ABC, CARS).

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Head-to-head comparisons of behavioral interventions (e.g., NDBI vs. traditional ABA) for long-term outcomes in diverse populations.
- **Effectiveness**: Real-world effectiveness of extended reality (XR) interventions in clinical settings, particularly for subgroups with severe sensory sensitivities.
- **Feasibility**: Long-term feasibility and scalability of ketogenic diet interventions combined with rehabilitation in community-based care.
- **Association**: Neurophysiological markers (e.g., sleep spindle characteristics) as predictors of treatment response or disease progression.
- **Effectiveness**: Longitudinal studies on the durability of motor skill improvements from exercise interventions beyond short-term follow-up.

## Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, meta-analysis: “Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD.” [1]
2. **Hao et al. (2026)**, systematic review: “There was a significant increase in XR autism research after 2020, with virtual reality (VR) as the largest modality (n = 61, 53.0%), followed by augmented reality (AR) (n = 36, 31.3%).” [8]
3. **Restoy et al. (2024)**, meta-analysis: “Studies of non-pharmacological interventions showed significant improvement in both ER and ED.” [5]
4. **Sandbank et al. (2023)**, meta-analysis: “When effects were then restricted to exclude those at high risk of detection bias, only one significant summary effect was estimated-naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions on measures of diagnostic characteristics of autism (0.30, 0.03 to 0.57; P=0.03).” [4]
5. **Zeidan et al. (2022)**, systematic review: “Prevalence estimates increased over time and varied greatly within and across sociodemographic groups.” [10]

## Conflicting evidence
None was retrieved.

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Head-to-head comparisons of behavioral interventions (e.g., NDBI vs. traditional ABA) for long-term social communication outcomes.
- **Effectiveness**: Real-world implementation of extended reality (XR) interventions in diverse clinical settings.
- **Feasibility**: Scalability of caregiver-assisted group-based interventions for nonverbal developmental gains.
- **Association**: Longitudinal studies on sleep spindle characteristics as biomarkers for ASD progression.
- **Dose-response**: Optimal duration/intensity of exercise interventions for sustained motor skill improvements in ASD.

## Evidence limitations

- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses appear only where an FDA label gives them for this condition (section 2.1); for any other use, see a prescribing source. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 3 retrieved record(s) were excluded because they were not about the question's subject.
- The web search timed out, so it may be incomplete; the absence of evidence cannot be concluded from it.
- The guideline-organisation search did not respond fully, so it may be incomplete; the absence of evidence cannot be concluded from it.
- **Drug landscape is incomplete because a regulatory/drug source failed or was only partly retrieved; this does not show that no drug exists.**
