## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports **exercise interventions** (e.g., community-based dance or structured physical activity) to improve motor skills in children with autism, and **naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions (NDBIs)**—especially caregiver-assisted group programs—to enhance developmental and social outcomes. A **modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation** showed short-term reductions in behavioral and symptom severity, but this remains a specialized approach. **No evidence was found for pharmacologic or feeding interventions in this population.**

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills. [1]

**Immediate context**
Direct evidence supports **behavioral interventions (NDBIs)** and **exercise** for motor and developmental gains, but **no guideline-recommended pharmacologic or feeding treatments** were retrieved. The ketogenic diet showed short-term behavioral benefits but lacks long-term data. Ongoing trials explore vitamin B6 for sensory reactivity and dance programs for motor skills.

## Latest findings
- **2026 Dec**: Observational study found that sleep spindle characteristics (amplitude, number, density) were significantly associated with autism spectrum disorder in children [2].
- **2026 Dec**: Case study reported sustained core symptom reduction in a 12-month follow-up of a child with severe autism after bilateral dual-target deep brain stimulation [3].
- **2026-09-23**: Pilot randomized controlled trial showed caregiver-assisted group-based Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Intervention (NDBI) improved developmental and social outcomes in autistic preschoolers [4].
- **2026-09-11**: Randomized controlled trial found a modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation training reduced behavioral and core symptom scores in young children with ASD compared to rehabilitation alone [5].

## Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, meta-analysis: “Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD.” [1]
2. **Hao et al. (2026)**, systematic review: “There was a significant increase in XR autism research after 2020, with virtual reality (VR) as the largest modality (n = 61, 53.0%), followed by augmented reality (AR) (n = 36, 31.3%).” [7]
3. **Sandbank et al. (2023)**, meta-analysis: “When effects were then restricted to exclude those at high risk of detection bias, only one significant summary effect was estimated-naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions on measures of diagnostic characteristics of autism (0.30, 0.03 to 0.57; P=0.03).” [6]
4. **Zeidan et al. (2022)**, systematic review: “Prevalence estimates increased over time and varied greatly within and across sociodemographic groups.” [8]
5. **Hume et al. (2021)**, systematic review: “Combined with the previous review, 972 articles were synthesized, from which the authors found 28 focused intervention practices that met the criteria for evidence-based practice (EBP).” [9]

## Conflicting evidence
None was retrieved.

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Head-to-head comparisons of behavioral interventions (e.g., NDBI vs. traditional therapy) for long-term social communication outcomes.
- **Effectiveness**: Real-world effectiveness of extended reality (XR) interventions in diverse clinical settings.
- **Feasibility**: Long-term feasibility and scalability of deep brain stimulation for severe autism symptoms.
- **Association**: Exploring the neurophysiological markers (e.g., sleep spindle characteristics) as potential predictors of treatment response.
- **Associations**: Investigating the role of sensory reactivity in response to vitamin B6 supplementation (NCT06234501, recruiting).

## Evidence limitations

- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses appear only where an FDA label gives them for this condition (section 2.1); for any other use, see a prescribing source. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 1 retrieved record(s) were excluded because they were not about the question's subject.
- The PubMed / Europe PMC search timed out, so it may be incomplete; the absence of evidence cannot be concluded from it.
