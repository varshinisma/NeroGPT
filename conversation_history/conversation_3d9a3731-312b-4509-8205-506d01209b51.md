How to treat autism in children?

## Assistant

**Evidence searched on 2026-10-07** (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA drug labels, web)

**Bottom line**
Exercise interventions (e.g., dance, occupational therapy) show promise for improving motor skills in children with autism, with dose-response effects reported in recent meta-analyses (Wang et al., *European Journal of Pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42758334]).

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills (*Wang et al., European Journal of Pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42758334]-09-18).

**Immediate context**
Motor impairments are prevalent in ASD and linked to functional independence, while structured interventions like dance or occupational therapy may offer scalable, community-based solutions. However, social communication and emotion regulation remain areas where evidence is still emerging.

## Deep research

### Latest findings
**2026**
- **Modified ketogenic diet**: A **randomized controlled trial** (Liu et al., *BMC Pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42763398]) reported **short-term reductions** in behavioral (ABC) and diagnostic (CARS) severity scores after 2 months, but **no long-term follow-up** or head-to-head comparisons with standard care.
- **Deep brain stimulation (DBS)**: A **12-month case study** (Shi et al., *Clinical Neurophysiology*, 2026 [PMID 42759099]) showed **sustained reductions** in core ASD symptoms (e.g., ATEC down 81.3%, RBS-R down 96.4%) with no adverse events, but **no randomized data** or generalizability beyond one patient.
- **Behavioral sleep interventions**: A **systematic review** (Mazurek et al., *Sleep Medicine*, 2026 [PMID 42685447]) identified **39 studies** (2002–2026) supporting behavioral approaches for sleep problems in autistic children, though **no single intervention was consistently effective** across studies.

---

### Current evidence
**Evidence-supported but limited**
- **Motor skills**: Exercise interventions are supported by limited evidence to improve motor skills in ASD (Wang et al., 2026 [PMID 42758334]), supported by a **consistent multilevel meta-analysis** of direct evidence.

- **Feeding difficulties**: A **comprehensive review** (Fisberg et al., *Jornal de Pediatria*, 2026 [PMID 42716484]) proposed the **FEEDSETUP algorithm** for pediatric feeding difficulties, but this was **indirect evidence** (different population: broader feeding spectrum, not ASD-specific).

**Emerging**
- **Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Interventions (NDBIs)**: A **pilot RCT** (Seng et al., *Autism*, 2026 [PMID 42494078]) found **promising feasibility** for caregiver-assisted group-based NDBI, with **mixed efficacy** (social improvements in one-on-one groups only).
- **Extended Reality (XR)**: A **systematic review** (Hao et al., *International Journal of Medical Informatics*, 2026 [PMID 42784985]) highlighted **growing interest** in VR/AR for ASD, but **no direct efficacy data** was synthesized.
- **Sleep interventions**: Behavioral strategies are **emerging** as a **limitedly supported** approach (Mazurek et al., 2026 [PMID 42685447]), but **no single intervention was universally effective**.

**Experimental or preliminary**
- **Deep brain stimulation (DBS)**: A **single-case report** (Shi et al., 2026 [PMID 42759099]) demonstrated **dramatic symptom reduction**, but **no randomized or multi-patient data**.
- **Ketogenic diet**: A **short-term RCT** (Liu et al., 2026 [PMID 42763398]) showed **behavioral improvements**, but **no long-term or mechanistic clarity**.

**Indirect evidence**
- **Migrant families**: A **qualitative review** (Allport et al., *Global Health Action*, 2026 [PMID 42839840]) discussed **culturally coordinated support**, but this was **not ASD-specific** (different population: migrant heritage families).

**Protocol – no results**
- **Dance programme** (Chadha et al., *BMJ Open*, 2026 [PMID 42711081]), **music therapy** (Chen et al., *BMJ Open*, 2026 [PMID 42486527]), and **occupational therapy in water** (Kemp et al., *BMJ Open*, 2026 [PMID 42552003]) are **under investigation**; no efficacy established.

**Insufficient evidence**
- **Social communication**: No **direct evidence** (e.g., RCTs or meta-analyses) supports interventions for social communication in ASD from retrieved data.
- **Core symptoms**: Only **one case study** (DBS) and **no randomized trials** address core ASD symptoms.

---
### Non-drug treatments
- **Exercise**: supported by limited evidence for motor skills (Wang et al., 2026 [PMID 42758334]).
- **Behavioral sleep interventions**: **Emerging** support (Mazurek et al., 2026 [PMID 42685447]), but **no consensus**.
- **NDBIs**: **Preliminary efficacy** (Seng et al., 2026 [PMID 42494078]), but **group-based vs. one-on-one discrepancies**.
- **XR/VR**: **Exploratory interest** (Hao et al., 2026 [PMID 42784985]), **no direct efficacy data**.
- **Dietary interventions**: **Short-term behavioral improvements** (Liu et al., 2026 [PMID 42763398]), but **no long-term or mechanistic validation**.

---

### Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, meta-analysis: “Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD.” (PMID 42758334)
2. **Hao et al. (2026)**, systematic review: “There was a significant increase in XR autism research after 2020, with virtual reality (VR) as the largest modality (n = 61, 53.0%), followed by augmented reality (AR) (n = 36, 31.3%).” (PMID 42784985)
3. **Mazurek et al. (2026)**, systematic review: “Most studies (95%) found that behavioral interventions were effective for at least some sleep outcomes.” (PMID 42685447)
4. **Restoy et al. (2024)**, meta-analysis: “Studies of non-pharmacological interventions showed significant improvement in both ER and ED.” (PMID 38401510)
5. **Sandbank et al. (2023)**, meta-analysis: “When effects were then restricted to exclude those at high risk of detection bias, only one significant summary effect was estimated-naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions on measures of diagnostic characteristics of autism (0.30, 0.03 to 0.57; P=0.03).” (PMID 37963634)

### Conflicting or negative evidence
**None retrieved** for direct contradictions. However:
- **Exercise vs. NDBIs**: Wang et al. (2026) supports motor skills, while Seng et al. (2026) highlights **social communication limitations** in group-based NDBIs—**no direct comparison**.
- **Ketogenic diet**: Liu et al. (2026) reports **short-term benefits**, but **no long-term data** or placebo-controlled follow-up.

---
### What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: **No randomized trials** for social communication, core ASD symptoms, or long-term outcomes (e.g., ketogenic diet, DBS).
- **Effectiveness**: **No real-world effectiveness data** (e.g., how NDBIs perform outside pilot settings).
- **Feasibility**: **Group-based interventions** (e.g., dance, occupational therapy) show **promising accessibility** but **inconsistent efficacy**.
- **Association**: **Observational studies** (e.g., sleep spindles in Erçelebi et al., 2026 [PMID 42753649]) suggest **neurophysiological markers**, but **no causal links to treatment**.
- **Gaps**:
  - **No head-to-head trials** (e.g., exercise vs. NDBIs).
