## Assistant

**Bottom line** – No medication is currently approved to treat the core social‑communication deficits of autism spectrum disorder (ASD). The only agents with regulatory approval are antipsychotics (risperidone, aripiprazole) for irritability associated with ASD, and they are used to manage aggression, self‑injury, or severe repetitive behaviors. Adjunctive supplements (e.g., folinic acid, omega‑3 fatty acids) have been studied but lack consistent efficacy and are not approved for ASD (see evidence below).  

**Evidence searched on 2026‑10‑06**  

**Most recent finding (2026)** – In a randomized trial of 5‑17‑year‑olds with emotional dysregulation (including participants with ASD traits), a combination of omega‑3 fatty acids plus inositol was compared with N‑acetylcysteine; the study reported no clear superiority for either arm in reducing mood symptoms (PMID 42462928, 2026).  

**Immediate context** – Pharmacologic strategies for ASD focus on treating comorbid or disruptive behaviors rather than the core autistic phenotype. Guidelines continue to recommend risperidone or aripiprazole for irritability, while evidence for dietary supplements remains mixed and often limited to small or retracted trials.
