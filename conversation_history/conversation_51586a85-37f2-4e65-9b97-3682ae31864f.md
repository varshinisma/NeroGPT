## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports **exercise interventions** (improving motor skills) and **behavioral interventions** (e.g., naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions) for children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). A **modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation** showed short-term reductions in behavioral and symptom severity, but broader efficacy remains under investigation.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills. [1]

**Immediate context**
Behavioral interventions like caregiver-assisted group-based naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions (NDBIs) show promise for early developmental improvements, but their long-term effects are not yet established. Dietary approaches (e.g., modified ketogenic diet) demonstrate short-term symptom reduction but lack robust, long-term evidence. No evidence was retrieved on pharmacological treatments or social communication interventions.

## Latest findings
- **2026 Dec**: Systematic review found behavioral sleep interventions significantly improved sleep outcomes in autistic children under 18 [2].
- **2026 Dec**: Meta-analysis showed exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified [1].
- **2026-09-18**: RCT demonstrated a modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation training reduced ABC and CARS scores more than rehabilitation alone in young children with ASD [3].

## Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, meta-analysis: “Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD.” [1]
2. **Hao et al. (2026)**, systematic review: “There was a significant increase in XR autism research after 2020, with virtual reality (VR) as the largest modality (n = 61, 53.0%), followed by augmented reality (AR) (n = 36, 31.3%).” [5]
3. **Mazurek et al. (2026)**, systematic review: “Most studies (95%) found that behavioral interventions were effective for at least some sleep outcomes.” [2]
4. **Sandbank et al. (2023)**, meta-analysis: “When effects were then restricted to exclude those at high risk of detection bias, only one significant summary effect was estimated-naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions on measures of diagnostic characteristics of autism (0.30, 0.03 to 0.57; P=0.03).” [7]
5. **Zeidan et al. (2022)**, systematic review: “Prevalence estimates increased over time and varied greatly within and across sociodemographic groups.” [8]

## Conflicting evidence
None was retrieved.

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Head-to-head comparisons of behavioral sleep interventions vs. pharmacological sleep aids in ASD children.
- **Effectiveness**: Long-term outcomes of modified ketogenic diet interventions beyond 2 months.
- **Feasibility**: Scalability of caregiver-assisted group-based NDBIs in resource-limited settings.
- **Association**: Neurophysiological markers (e.g., sleep spindles) as predictors of response to behavioral interventions.
- **Dose-response**: Optimal frequency/duration of exercise interventions for sustained motor skill improvements.

## Evidence limitations

- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses appear only where an FDA label gives them for this condition (section 2.1); for any other use, see a prescribing source. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 3 retrieved record(s) were excluded because they were not about the question's subject.
