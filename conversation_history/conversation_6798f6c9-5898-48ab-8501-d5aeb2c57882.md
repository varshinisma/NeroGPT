## Assistant

**Evidence searched on 2026-10-07** (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA drug labels, web)

**Bottom line**
Exercise interventions (e.g., dance, occupational therapy) show evidence improving motor skills in children with autism, with dose-response effects for overall and gross motor function (Wang et al., *European journal of pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42758334]). Structured programs like music therapy and water competency training are under investigation for broader developmental outcomes, though no direct efficacy data is yet available.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills (Wang et al., *European journal of pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42758334]).

**Immediate context**
Motor impairments are prevalent in children with ASD and represent a key barrier to functional independence. While exercise shows promise, ongoing trials explore interventions like music therapy and occupational therapy for social communication and safety, though results remain unpublished.

## Deep research

### Latest findings
**2026**
- **Exercise**: A dose-response meta-analysis (Wang et al., *European Journal of Pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42758334]) found significant improvements in **motor skills** (overall, gross, and fine) in children with ASD, with dose-response (exploratory) effects for overall and gross motor skills.
- **Modified ketogenic diet**: A randomized controlled trial (Liu et al., *BMC Pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42763398]) reported **short-term reductions in ABC and CARS scores** (measures of behavioral and core ASD symptoms) when combined with rehabilitation, compared to rehabilitation alone. Feasibility was acceptable.
- **Behavioral sleep interventions**: A systematic review (Mazurek et al., *Sleep Medicine*, 2026 [PMID 42685447]) identified **39 studies** (2002–2026) supporting behavioral approaches for sleep problems in autistic children, though no single intervention was highlighted.
- **Extended reality (XR)**: A review (Hao et al., *International Journal of Medical Informatics*, 2026 [PMID 42784985]) noted **growing research** on VR/AR for ASD, with hybrid systems and training/educational modalities most common.
- **Deep brain stimulation (DBS)**: A 12-month case study (Shi et al., *Clinical Neurophysiology*, 2026 [PMID 42759099]) reported **sustained reductions in core ASD symptoms** (ATEC, RBS-R, AuBC, CARS-2 scores) with bilateral dual-target DBS, no adverse events.

### Current evidence
**Evidence-supported but limited**
- **Behavioral sleep interventions**: Systematic review (Mazurek et al., *Sleep Medicine*, 2026 [PMID 42685447]) reports behavioral approaches improve sleep in autistic children (39 studies, 2002–2026).

- **Exercise**: Meta-analysis (Wang et al., *European Journal of Pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42758334]) shows **motor skill improvements** in ASD, but dose-response specifics require validation.
- **Modified ketogenic diet**: RCT (Liu et al., *BMC Pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42763398]) demonstrates **short-term behavioral symptom reduction** (ABC/CARS scores) vs. rehabilitation alone, but long-term efficacy and mechanisms unclear.
- **Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Interventions (NDBIs)**: Pilot RCT (Seng et al., *Autism*, 2026 [PMID 42494078]) suggests **caregiver-assisted group-based NDBIs** improve developmental/social outcomes in preschoolers, though one-on-one benefits were greater.

**Emerging**
- **Extended reality (XR)**: Review (Hao et al., 2026 [PMID 42784985]) highlights **promising but heterogeneous** XR applications (VR/AR) for ASD, lacking standardized protocols or efficacy data.

**Experimental or preliminary**
- **Sleep spindle characteristics**: Observational study (Erçelebi et al., *Clinical Neurophysiology*, 2026 [PMID 42753649]) links **reduced spindle amplitude/number** to ASD, suggesting a potential neurophysiological marker (not a treatment).
- **Neuroimaging biomarkers**: Study (Lee et al., *Psychiatry Research: Neuroimaging*, 2026 [PMID 42691573]) identifies **GNN-based brain connectivity patterns** in ASD, but no therapeutic implication.

**Indirect evidence**
- **Feeding difficulties (FEEDSETUP)**: Review (Fisberg et al., *Jornal de Pediatria*, 2026 [PMID 42716484]) proposes a **structured feeding evaluation/treatment algorithm** for pediatric feeding disorders (not ASD-specific).

**Protocol – no results**
- **Dance program (AllPlay Dance)**: Protocol (Chadha et al., *BMJ Open*, 2026 [PMID 42711081]) for a community-based dance intervention (ANZCTR12625000600448).
- **Music therapy dosage**: Protocol (Chen et al., *BMJ Open*, 2026 [PMID 42486527]) for a systematic review/meta-analysis on music therapy’s effect on **social communication/emotion regulation** (Prospero CRD420251063906).

**Insufficient evidence**
- **Prenatal antiseizure medication**: Case-control (Hernández-Díaz et al., *NEJM*, 2024 [PMID 38507750]) links **topiramate/valproate exposure** to higher ASD risk (6.2–10.5% incidence) vs. no exposure (4.2%), but **not a treatment**.

- **Behavioral sleep interventions**: Systematic support for sleep improvements (Mazurek et al., 2026 [PMID 42685447]).
