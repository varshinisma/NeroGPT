## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports **structured exercise (e.g., dance, occupational therapy-based water interventions) to improve motor skills** in children with autism, with dose-response effects reported for overall and gross motor function. **Feeding difficulties can be addressed with structured, individualized treatment pathways**, though this evidence is drawn from broader pediatric feeding populations rather than ASD-specific studies. **No direct evidence yet supports interventions for core social communication or emotion regulation** in the retrieved data.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills [1].

**Immediate context**
Structured physical interventions (e.g., dance, water therapy) show promise for motor skill enhancement, but **no direct evidence exists for social communication or emotion regulation** in the retrieved studies. Feeding difficulties are addressed via structured protocols, though these are not ASD-specific. **No efficacy data** is available for core autism symptoms from the current evidence.

## 1. Treatment approaches and strength of evidence

| Approach                          | Evidence level                     | What the evidence shows                                                                                     | Reference                     |

| Caregiver-assisted NDBI           | Emerging                          | Caregiver-assisted group-based Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Interventions (NDBIs) showed promising improvements in developmental and social outcomes in preschoolers. | [3]              |

### Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, Meta-Analysis: Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified [1].
2. **Liu et al. (2026)**, Randomized Controlled Trial: A modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation training showed greater short-term reductions in ABC and CARS scores than rehabilitation alone [2].
3. **Seng et al. (2026)**, Randomized Controlled Trial: Caregiver-assisted group-based NDBI demonstrated promising developmental and social improvements in autistic preschoolers [3].
4. **Shi et al. (2026)**, Case Reports: Bilateral dual-target deep brain stimulation led to sustained reductions in core ASD symptoms over 12 months in a single pediatric case [6].
5. **Mazurek et al. (2026)**, Systematic Review: Behavioral sleep interventions for autistic children were studied in 39 published studies, though no efficacy outcomes were explicitly reported [5].

## 4. Evidence gaps and limitations

### Conflicting or negative evidence
None was retrieved.

### What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Head-to-head trials comparing exercise, ketogenic diet, and behavioral interventions for motor, social, or core symptom outcomes in ASD.
- **Feasibility**: Long-term adherence and scalability of caregiver-assisted NDBIs in community settings.
- **Association**: Neurophysiological markers (e.g., sleep spindle characteristics) as predictors of response to behavioral or pharmacological interventions.
- **Effectiveness**: Real-world implementation of extended reality (XR) interventions in clinical practice for ASD symptom management.
- **Exploratory**: Potential of deep brain stimulation (DBS) as a scalable treatment for severe ASD, beyond single-case reports.

### Limitations of this report
- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses are not extracted in this report: see the linked FDA labels. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 2 retrieved record(s) were excluded because they were not about the question's subject.
- The PubMed / Europe PMC search timed out, so it may be incomplete; the absence of evidence cannot be concluded from it.
