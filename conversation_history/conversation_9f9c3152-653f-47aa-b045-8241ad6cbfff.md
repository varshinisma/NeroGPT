## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports **structured physical activity interventions (e.g., dance, occupational therapy, and exercise programs)** to improve motor skills in children with autism, with dose-response effects established for overall and gross motor function. **No direct evidence** was found for treatments addressing core social communication, emotion regulation, or behavioral symptoms, though protocols for music therapy and structured walking interventions are under investigation.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills. [1]

**Immediate context**
Direct evidence is limited to **motor skill interventions** (e.g., exercise, dance, occupational therapy), while protocols for social communication or behavioral treatments (e.g., music therapy) remain exploratory. No established treatments for core ASD symptoms were retrieved.

## 1. Treatment approaches and strength of evidence

| Approach | Evidence level | What the evidence shows | Reference |
|----------|----------------|------------------------|-----------|
| Exercise interventions (multilevel meta-analysis) | Evidence-supported but limited | Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships for overall and gross motor skills. | [1] |
| Modified ketogenic diet + rehabilitation training | Evidence-supported but limited | The intervention was associated with greater short-term reductions in ABC and CARS scores (measuring behavior and autism severity) compared to rehabilitation alone in young children with ASD. | [2] |
| Caregiver-assisted group-based Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Intervention (NDBI) | Emerging | Promising preliminary efficacy in improving developmental and social outcomes in autistic preschoolers, especially in accessible group settings. | [3] |
| Extended Reality (XR) interventions (systematic review) | Emerging | XR (e.g., VR, AR) shows potential for supporting children with autism, with hybrid systems and training/educational modalities being the most studied. | [4] |
| Behavioral sleep interventions (systematic review) | Emerging | Multiple behavioral interventions have been evaluated for sleep problems in autistic children, though no single approach is universally endorsed. | [5] |
| Bilateral dual-target deep brain stimulation (case study) | Experimental or preliminary | Sustained reduction in core ASD symptoms (e.g., ATEC, RBS-R scores) over 12 months in a single pediatric case with no adverse events. | [6] |
| Music therapy (protocol for systematic review/meta-analysis) | Protocol - no results | Under investigation; no efficacy data yet for music therapy on social communication or emotion regulation in ASD. | [7] |
| Occupational therapy-based water competency intervention (protocol) | Protocol - no results | Under investigation; aims to improve water safety skills in children with ASD via occupational therapy. | [8] |

### Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, Meta-Analysis: Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills [1].
2. **Liu et al. (2026)**, Randomized Controlled Trial: A modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation training showed greater short-term reductions in ABC and CARS scores compared to rehabilitation alone [2].
3. **Seng et al. (2026)**, Randomized Controlled Trial: Caregiver-assisted group-based NDBI improved developmental and social outcomes in autistic preschoolers, with nonverbal gains in one-on-one subgroups [3].
4. **Shi et al. (2026)**, Case Reports: Bilateral dual-target deep brain stimulation led to sustained reductions in core ASD symptoms (e.g., ATEC, RBS-R scores) over 12 months in a single pediatric case [6].
5. **Mazurek et al. (2026)**, Systematic Review: Behavioral sleep interventions for autistic children show variability in effectiveness, with no consensus on optimal approaches [5].

## 4. Evidence gaps and limitations

### Conflicting or negative evidence
None was retrieved.

### What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Music therapy’s impact on social communication and emotion regulation in ASD (protocol for systematic review/meta-analysis; no results yet).
- **Effectiveness**: Occupational therapy-based water competency interventions for improving safety skills in autistic children (protocol only; no efficacy data).
- **Feasibility**: Extended reality (XR) interventions (e.g., VR/AR) for autism support, though preliminary data suggest potential (systematic review identified gaps in rigorous trial designs).
- **Association**: Altered sleep spindle characteristics as a neurophysiological marker for ASD (observational study; no causal evidence).
- **Exploratory**: Dual-target deep brain stimulation’s broader applicability beyond single-case studies (only one pediatric case reported).

### Limitations of this report
- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses appear only where an FDA label gives them for this condition (section 2.1); for any other use, see a prescribing source. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 1 retrieved record(s) were excluded because they were not about the question's subject.
- The PubMed / Europe PMC search timed out, so it may be incomplete; the absence of evidence cannot be concluded from it.
