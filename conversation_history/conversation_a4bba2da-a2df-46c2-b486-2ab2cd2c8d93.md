## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports **structured exercise and motor-focused interventions** (e.g., dance, occupational therapy) to improve motor skills in children with autism, with dose-response effects reported for overall and gross motor function. **Feeding difficulties** can be addressed with structured, individualized treatment pathways, though evidence is indirect for autism-specific protocols. **Social communication and emotion regulation** remain under investigation, with no established treatments yet reported.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills [1].

**Immediate context**
Direct evidence supports **motor skill interventions** (e.g., dance, occupational therapy) but does not yet establish broader autism symptom management (e.g., social communication). Feeding difficulties are addressed via structured protocols, though these are not autism-specific. No treatments for core autism symptoms (e.g., social communication) are reported in the retrieved data.

## Latest findings
- **2026-12**: **Case study**, Dual-target deep brain stimulation (DBS) in a 12-month-old child with severe autism showed sustained reductions in core symptoms (ATEC, RBS-R, AuBC, CARS-2 scores) over 12 months with no adverse events [2].
- **2026-12**: **Systematic review**, Behavioral interventions for sleep problems in autistic children demonstrated consistent improvements in sleep outcomes across 39 studies [3].
- **2026-09-22**: **Case study**, Crohn’s disease in an adolescent with autism was misdiagnosed due to communication barriers, highlighting the need for gastrointestinal screening in ASD [4].
- **2026-09-18**: **Meta-analysis**, Exercise interventions improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response effects identified [1].

## Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, Meta-analysis: Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response effects [1].
2. **Liu et al. (2026)**, Randomized controlled trial: A modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation training reduced ABC and CARS scores more than rehabilitation alone in young children with ASD [5].
3. **Seng et al. (2026)**, Randomized controlled trial: Caregiver-assisted group-based NDBI improved developmental and social outcomes in preschoolers with ASD [6].
4. **Mazurek et al. (2026)**, Systematic review: Behavioral sleep interventions improved sleep outcomes in autistic children, supported by 39 studies [3].

## Conflicting evidence
None was retrieved.

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Head-to-head trials comparing exercise, diet, and behavioral interventions for motor, social, and core symptom outcomes in ASD.
- **Effectiveness**: Long-term feasibility and real-world implementation of caregiver-assisted NDBI and XR interventions in diverse clinical settings.
- **Feasibility**: Scalability of dual-target DBS for broader ASD populations, including safety and cost-effectiveness over extended follow-up.
- **Association**: Neurophysiological markers (e.g., sleep spindle characteristics) as predictors of treatment response in behavioral or pharmacological interventions.
- **Effectiveness**: Impact of structured walking interventions on gait parameters and functional independence in ASD (NCT06659328, results pending).

## Evidence limitations

- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses appear only where an FDA label gives them for this condition (section 2.1); for any other use, see a prescribing source. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 1 retrieved record(s) were excluded because they were not about the question's subject.
- The PubMed / Europe PMC search timed out, so it may be incomplete; the absence of evidence cannot be concluded from it.
