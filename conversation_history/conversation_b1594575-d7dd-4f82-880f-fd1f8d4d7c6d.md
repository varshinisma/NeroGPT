## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports **exercise interventions** (improving motor skills) and **behavioral therapies** (e.g., naturalistic developmental interventions) as effective approaches for children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). A **modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation** showed short-term reductions in behavioral and symptom severity, but this remains a targeted, investigational strategy.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills [1].

**Immediate context**
Behavioral interventions (e.g., caregiver-assisted group-based naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions) are promising for early developmental gains, while dietary approaches like the modified ketogenic diet show preliminary behavioral benefits but lack long-term evidence. No broad-spectrum treatment is established for core ASD symptoms.

## Latest findings
- **2026 Dec**: Observational study [2] – Altered sleep spindle characteristics were significantly associated with autism spectrum disorder in children, with reduced spindle amplitude, number, and density.
- **2026 Dec**: Observational study [3] – A 12-month case study reported sustained reductions in core ASD symptoms (e.g., ATEC scores decreased by 81.3%) following bilateral dual-target deep brain stimulation.
- **2026-09-22**: Case study [4] – Crohn’s disease was misdiagnosed in an adolescent with ASD due to communication barriers, highlighting gastrointestinal symptom masking.
- **2026-09-18**: Meta-analysis [1] – Exercise interventions improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response effects identified.

## Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, Meta-analysis: Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response effects identified [1].
2. **Liu et al. (2026)**, Randomized controlled trial: A modified ketogenic diet + rehabilitation reduced ASD symptom severity (ABC/CARS scores) more than rehabilitation alone after 2 months [6].
3. **Seng et al. (2026)**, Randomized controlled trial: Caregiver-assisted group-based NDBIs showed early developmental improvements, though nonverbal gains were limited [5].
4. **Sikich et al. (2021)**, Randomized controlled trial (Phase II): Intranasal oxytocin did not significantly alter primary ABC-mSW scores vs. placebo in children/adolescents with ASD [8].

## Conflicting evidence
None was retrieved.

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Head-to-head comparisons of exercise vs. ketogenic diet interventions for motor/social outcomes in ASD (no direct trials retrieved).
- **Effectiveness**: Long-term feasibility and scalability of caregiver-assisted group-based NDBIs in community settings (follow-up >2 years not reported).
- **Feasibility**: Optimal dosage and delivery formats for music therapy to improve social communication in ASD (protocol-only evidence retrieved).
- **Association**: Neurophysiological markers (e.g., sleep spindles) as predictors of treatment response (observational only; causality not established).
- **Effectiveness**: Water competency interventions for ASD-related drowning risks (protocol-stage trial; no efficacy data).

## Evidence limitations

- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses appear only where an FDA label gives them for this condition (section 2.1); for any other use, see a prescribing source. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 1 retrieved record(s) were excluded because they were not about the question's subject.
- The PubMed / Europe PMC search timed out, so it may be incomplete; the absence of evidence cannot be concluded from it.
