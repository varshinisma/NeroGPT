## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports **behavioral interventions (e.g., naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions) and dietary approaches (e.g., modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation)** to improve core symptoms (e.g., social communication, repetitive behaviors) and motor skills in children with autism. Exercise interventions also show promise for motor function, but the evidence does not address broader cognitive or language outcomes.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills [1].

**Immediate context**
Behavioral interventions (e.g., caregiver-assisted group-based NDBI) and dietary modifications (e.g., modified ketogenic diet) show short-term efficacy for specific symptoms, but no evidence supports broad cognitive or language improvements. Motor-focused interventions (e.g., dance programs) are under investigation but not yet established.

## Latest findings
- **2026 Dec**: Systematic review found behavioral sleep interventions significantly improved sleep outcomes in autistic children under 18 [2].
- **2026-09-18**: Meta-analysis showed exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified [1].
- **2026-09-11**: Randomized controlled trial demonstrated a modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation training reduced ABC and CARS scores more than rehabilitation alone in young children with ASD [3].
- **2026-07-23**: Pilot randomized controlled trial showed caregiver-assisted group-based Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Intervention (NDBI) improved developmental and social outcomes in autistic preschoolers [4].

## Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, meta-analysis: “Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD.” [1]
2. **Hao et al. (2026)**, systematic review: “There was a significant increase in XR autism research after 2020, with virtual reality (VR) as the largest modality (n = 61, 53.0%), followed by augmented reality (AR) (n = 36, 31.3%).” [7]
3. **Mazurek et al. (2026)**, systematic review: “Most studies (95%) found that behavioral interventions were effective for at least some sleep outcomes.” [2]
4. **Floríndez et al. (2025)**, systematic review: “Only one study purposefully recruited autistic adults, and no studies included intervention elements tailored to race/ethnicity, culture, and/or socioeconomic status.” [9]
5. **Restoy et al. (2024)**, meta-analysis: “Studies of non-pharmacological interventions showed significant improvement in both ER and ED.” [6]

## Conflicting evidence
None was retrieved.

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Long-term effects of modified ketogenic diets in ASD (only short-term feasibility reported).
- **Effectiveness**: Real-world effectiveness of extended reality (XR) interventions for diverse autistic populations.
- **Feasibility**: Scalability of caregiver-assisted group-based NDBIs in resource-limited settings.
- **Association**: Potential dose-response relationships for pharmacological treatments beyond aripiprazole, atomoxetine, and bumetanide.
- **Effectiveness**: Head-to-head comparisons of behavioral sleep interventions vs. pharmacological sleep aids in autistic children.

## Evidence limitations

- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses appear only where an FDA label gives them for this condition (section 2.1); for any other use, see a prescribing source. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 2 retrieved record(s) were excluded because they were not about the question's subject.
