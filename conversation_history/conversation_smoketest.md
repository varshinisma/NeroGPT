**Bottom line:**  
Current migraine‑prevention practice is dominated by CGRP‑targeted monoclonal antibodies (erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab, eptinezumab) and onabotulinumtoxinA as first‑line options, with oral gepants such as atogepant emerging as a convenient newer alternative; traditional oral agents (e.g., beta‑blockers, antiepileptics) remain second‑line (Potrebic et al., *Neurology*, 2026) [PMID 42673560].

**Evidence searched on 2026‑10‑06**  

**Most recent finding (2026):** The American Academy of Neurology practice guideline (Oct 13 2026) recommends CGRP monoclonal antibodies and onabotulinumtoxinA as the preferred pharmacologic preventive therapies for adults with migraine (Potrebic et al., *Neurology*, 2026) [PMID 42673560].

**Immediate context:** Real‑world Medicare claims analysis published later in 2026 shows comparable treatment‑switching patterns and health‑care costs between onabotulinumtoxinA and CGRP‑mAbs, reinforcing their comparable effectiveness in chronic migraine (Dong et al., *J Med Econ*, 2026) [PMID 41725292]. A phase‑3 pediatric trial of the oral CGRP‑receptor antagonist atogepant is now recruiting, indicating expanding oral preventive options (NCT05711394, recruiting as of 2026‑10‑05).
