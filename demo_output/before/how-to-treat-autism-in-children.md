# How to treat autism in children?

**Evidence searched on 2026-10-06** (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA drug labels, web)

**Bottom line**
Exercise interventions show a dose-response relationship improving motor skills in children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD), with significant gains in overall, gross, and fine motor function (Wang et al., *European Journal of Pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42758334]).

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills. (Wang et al., *European Journal of Pediatrics*, 2026 [PMID 42758334])

**Immediate context**
Motor impairments are prevalent in children with ASD and may impact functional independence. While exercise shows promise for motor skills, **no direct evidence** from retrieved sources addresses social communication or behavioral symptoms. Ongoing trials explore interventions like occupational therapy and structured walking, but results are pending.

### Latest findings
**2026**
- **Behavioral sleep interventions**: A systematic review of 39 studies (2002–2026) reported **significant improvements in sleep outcomes** for autistic children under 18 using behavioral strategies (Mazurek et al., *Sleep Medicine*, 2026 [PMID 42685447]).
- **Oral care**: No new direct evidence on tailored oral hygiene interventions for autistic children was retrieved (Floríndez et al., *Autism*, 2025 [PMID 39056277]).
- **Nature-based activity**: Qualitative study (n=15 parents) reported **increased communication and calmness** in preschool autistic children during nature walks, though coordination challenges were noted (Galbraith et al., *International Journal of Qualitative Studies on Health and Well-being*, 2026 [PMID 42603141]).

---

### Current evidence
**Evidence-supported but limited**
- **Motor skills**: Exercise interventions (e.g., dance, structured walking) are **evidence-supported but limited** for improving motor skills in ASD, with dose-response (exploratory) effects (Wang et al., 2026 [PMID 42758334]).

**Emerging**
- **Sleep**: Behavioral interventions (e.g., bedtime routines, sleep restriction) are **evidence-supported but limited** for sleep problems in autistic children (Mazurek et al., 2026 [PMID 42685447]).
- **Emotion regulation**: Non-pharmacological therapies (e.g., CBT-adapted protocols) show **promising but inconsistent** effects on emotion dysregulation (Restoy et al., 2024 [PMID 38401510]).
- **Social communication**: Music therapy dosage remains **under investigation** (Chen et al., *BMJ Open*, 2026 [PMID 42486527] [protocol]; no results retrieved).

**Experimental or preliminary**
- **Oral hygiene**: Peer modeling and video demonstrations are **under investigation** for improving plaque control in autistic children (NCT07859696, *not yet recruiting*).

**Indirect evidence**
- **Genetic/environmental overlap**: Twin studies suggest **shared genetic/environmental factors** between autistic traits and anxiety/depression, but no direct ASD treatment implications (Han et al., *Journal of Affective Disorders*, 2026 [PMID 42508582]). *(indirect evidence: different or related population)*
- **Feeding difficulties**: A structured algorithm (FEEDSETUP) addresses pediatric feeding challenges in **related populations** (Fisberg et al., *Jornal de Pediatria*, 2026 [PMID 42716484]).

**Insufficient evidence**
- **Nutraceuticals**: Omega-3/inositol and N-acetylcysteine showed **trends toward benefit** for depression/mania in emotionally dysregulated youth *with or without ASD* (Wozniak et al., *Journal of Affective Disorders*, 2026 [PMID 42462928]); **not specific to ASD**.

**Protocol – no results**
- Dance programs (AllPlay Dance; Chadha et al., *BMJ Open*, 2026 [PMID 42711081]), music therapy (Chen et al., 2026 [PMID 42486527]), and water safety (Kemp et al., 2026 [PMID 42552003]) are **under investigation**; no efficacy established.

- **Nature-based activities**: Parent-reported benefits in communication/calmness (Galbraith et al., 2026 [PMID 42603141]).
- **Oral hygiene**: Peer modeling interventions **under investigation** (NCT07859696).
