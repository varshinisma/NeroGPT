# How to treat autism in children?

**Evidence searched on 2026-10-07** (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA drug labels, web)

## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports **structured exercise interventions (e.g., dance, occupational therapy-based water programs) to improve motor skills** in children with autism, with dose-response effects reported for overall and gross motor function. **Feeding difficulties can be addressed with a structured, individualized treatment algorithm**, though this evidence is indirect (not specific to autism). **No direct evidence was found for social communication or emotion regulation interventions**, and no efficacy data were posted for ongoing trials.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills. [1]

**Immediate context**
Structured physical interventions (e.g., dance, water-based therapy) show direct evidence for motor skill improvement, but **no direct evidence supports interventions for social communication or emotion regulation**. Feeding difficulties are addressed by a structured algorithm, though this is indirect evidence (not autism-specific). No efficacy results are available for ongoing trials.

## 1. Treatment approaches and strength of evidence

| Approach | Evidence level | What the evidence shows | Reference |
|----------|----------------|------------------------|-----------|
| Exercise interventions (e.g., dance, structured walking) | Evidence-supported but limited | Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified for overall and gross motor skills [1]. | [1] |
| Modified ketogenic diet + rehabilitation training | Evidence-supported but limited | The modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation training was associated with greater short-term reductions in ABC and CARS scores (autism severity measures) compared to rehabilitation alone [2]. | [2] |
| Caregiver-assisted group-based Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Intervention (NDBI) | Emerging | Caregiver-assisted group-based NDBI showed promising improvements in developmental and social outcomes in autistic preschoolers, though nonverbal development improvements were limited to one-on-one interventions [3]. | [3] |
| Extended reality (XR) interventions (e.g., virtual reality) | Emerging | XR interventions, particularly virtual reality, show potential for supporting children with autism, though the review highlights a lack of standardized outcomes or long-term efficacy data [4]. | [4] |
| Behavioral sleep interventions | Evidence-supported but limited | Behavioral interventions consistently improve sleep outcomes in autistic children, though the review notes variability in study designs and outcomes [5]. | [5] |
| Deep brain stimulation (DBS) for severe pediatric autism | Experimental or preliminary | A 12-month case study reported sustained reductions in core ASD symptoms (e.g., ATEC, RBS-R scores) with bilateral dual-target DBS, though this was a single-case study with no adverse events [6]. | [6] |
| Feeding difficulties treatment (FEEDSETUP) | Indirect evidence (different or related population) | FEEDSETUP offers a structured, evidence-based approach to pediatric feeding difficulties, though it was not specifically validated for ASD [7]. | [7] |

### Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, Meta-Analysis: Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified [1].
2. **Liu et al. (2026)**, Randomized Controlled Trial: The modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation training showed greater short-term reductions in autism severity scores (ABC, CARS) than rehabilitation alone [2].
3. **Seng et al. (2026)**, Randomized Controlled Trial: Caregiver-assisted group-based NDBI improved developmental and social outcomes in autistic preschoolers, though nonverbal gains were limited to one-on-one interventions [3].
4. **Shi et al. (2026)**, Case Reports: Bilateral dual-target deep brain stimulation sustained reductions in core ASD symptoms (e.g., ATEC, RBS-R) over 12 months in a single pediatric case [6].
5. **Mazurek et al. (2026)**, Systematic Review: Behavioral sleep interventions consistently improved sleep outcomes in autistic children, though study designs varied [5].

## 4. Evidence gaps and limitations

### Conflicting or negative evidence
None was retrieved.

### What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: The long-term effects of extended reality (XR) interventions (e.g., virtual reality) for autism symptoms remain unclear, as current evidence is limited to short-term or exploratory studies [4].
- **Feasibility**: The feasibility of caregiver-assisted group-based NDBI in resource-limited settings requires further investigation, particularly for nonverbal developmental outcomes [3].
- **Association**: The neurophysiological markers (e.g., sleep spindle characteristics) in ASD are under investigation as potential biomarkers, though their clinical utility is not yet established [8].
- **Effectiveness**: The effectiveness of structured walking interventions (NCT06659328) for gait parameters in ASD is pending, as results have not yet been posted.
- **Associations**: The role of gut microbiota (e.g., *Lactobacillus plantarum* PS128) in improving social responsiveness in ASD was investigated in a terminated trial (NCT05307744), with results posted but no clear conclusions due to operational challenges.

### Limitations of this report
- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses are not extracted in this report: see the linked FDA labels. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 1 retrieved record(s) were excluded because they were not about the question's subject.
- The PubMed / Europe PMC search timed out, so it may be incomplete; the absence of evidence cannot be concluded from it.
