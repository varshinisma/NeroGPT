# How do you diagnose PANDAs?

**Evidence searched on 2026-10-14** (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA drug labels, web)

## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
Diagnosing PANDAS (Pediatric Autoimmune Neuropsychiatric Disorders Associated with Streptococcal Infections) currently relies on identifying **acute onset of obsessive-compulsive symptoms or tic disorders** alongside **evidence of streptococcal infection** (e.g., elevated ASOT titers, recent streptococcal throat infection) and **exclusion of other causes** (e.g., ASD, other tic disorders). Autoantibody screening (e.g., via nucleic acid programmable arrays) is an emerging but not yet standardized diagnostic tool, with 117 autoantibody targets identified in PANDAS patients distinct from ASD controls [1].

**Most important recent finding**
Autoantibody screening identified **117 unique autoantibody targets** exclusively in PANDAS patients compared to ASD controls and healthy children, suggesting potential biomarkers for diagnosis [1].

**Immediate context**
While clinical criteria (symptom onset, streptococcal link) form the diagnostic foundation, **no universally supported biomarkers or diagnostic algorithms** exist for PANDAS. Autoantibody testing remains investigational and not part of routine clinical practice.

## Latest findings

- **Autoantibody screening in pediatric autoimmune neuropsychiatric disorder associated with streptococcal infections (PANDAS)** (2026-04-29, **research-article**, *direct evidence*)
 A study of **children with PANDAS compared to patients with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and healthy controls** used unbiased autoantibody screening to identify 1,235 non-overlapping protein targets. **117 autoantibody targets were exclusively detected in PANDAS patients**, but profiles were highly heterogeneous, with no reliable diagnostic classifier due to cross-validation limitations. *[1]*

- **Immune dysregulation in pediatric tic disorders (including PANDAS)** (2026-03-10, **review-article**, *direct evidence*)
 This review synthesized evidence on **neuroimaging, genetics (e.g., IL-1RN gene variations), and biomarkers** in pediatric tic disorders, including PANDAS. **No single diagnostic biomarker was established**, but immune-targeted therapies (e.g., IVIG, plasmapheresis) were highlighted as emerging approaches. *[2]* *(indirect evidence: different or related population)*

- **Pathways to PANDAS diagnosis in the UK** (2026-05-18, **research-article**, *direct evidence*)
 **Children aged 2–18 years with functional tic-like behaviors (FTLBs)** were surveyed, revealing that **over half met PANDAS criteria** but faced delays, misdiagnosis (e.g., transient tics), and fragmented care. **No standardized diagnostic protocol was identified**, and private healthcare was accessed by 25% of diagnosed cases. *[3]* *(indirect evidence: different or related population)*

*(No additional direct evidence retrieved for PANDAS diagnosis beyond 2026-04-29.)*

## Conflicting evidence
No direct disagreement was retrieved between studies on PANDAS diagnostic criteria. While **Eremija et al. (2026)** identified heterogeneous autoantibody profiles in PANDAS patients, **Ludlow et al. (2026)** reported clinical ambiguity in diagnostic labeling (e.g., PANDAS vs. transient tics) without conflicting biomarker findings. No studies directly compared diagnostic accuracy or supported a single gold-standard approach.

## What remains under investigation
- **Diagnostic biomarkers**: No reliable autoantibody or biomarker panel has been supported by limited evidence for PANDAS, despite exploratory findings [1].
- **Neuroimaging correlates**: While basal ganglia changes are linked to tic disorders, their specificity for PANDAS remains unclear [2].
- **Clinical heterogeneity**: Subgroups (e.g., PANDAS vs. ASD overlap) require standardized diagnostic criteria to reduce mislabeling [3]. *(indirect evidence: different or related population)*
- **Longitudinal validation**: No prospective studies confirm whether early autoantibody detection predicts PANDAS progression or treatment response.
- **Immune profiling**: The role of *IL-1RN* gene variants and neuroinflammation in PANDAS diagnosis requires further mechanistic study [2]. *(indirect evidence: different or related population)*
- **Diagnostic algorithms**: No supported decision-support tools integrate clinical, immunological, or imaging data for PANDAS [1].
