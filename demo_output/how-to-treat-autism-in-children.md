# How to treat autism in children?

**Evidence searched on 2026-10-09** (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA drug labels, web)

## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports **intervention-based approaches** for autism in children, including **exercise programs** (reported to improve motor skills) and **feeding difficulty management** (structured, individualized care). No direct evidence yet supports social communication or emotion regulation interventions, though protocols exist. **FDA-approved drugs** (e.g., aripiprazole, risperidone) are available for irritability but are not covered by this data.

**Most important recent finding**
Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD, with dose-response relationships identified. [1]

**Immediate context**
While motor skill interventions and feeding difficulty protocols show promise, **no direct evidence** yet addresses core social communication or emotion regulation. FDA-approved drugs exist for irritability but are not part of this retrieved evidence.

## Latest findings

- **Extended reality (XR) interventions** (2026 Dec, advance publication, *Systematic Review*)
  A review of 115 studies found that virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) significantly improved engagement, social communication, emotional recognition, and adaptive functioning in children with autism. Most studies reported positive outcomes, though the review highlights variability in technical characteristics like avatar-based interaction and AI integration [2].

- **Modified ketogenic diet** (2026-09-11, *Randomized Controlled Trial*)
  In young children with autism, a modified ketogenic diet combined with rehabilitation training showed greater short-term reductions in ABC (Aberrant Behavior Checklist) and CARS (Childhood Autism Rating Scale) scores compared to rehabilitation alone. The intervention was feasible but limited to short-term follow-up [3].

- **Caregiver-assisted group-based NDBI** (2026-07-23, *Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial*)
  A pilot study found that caregiver-assisted group-based Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Interventions (NDBI) improved language, social interaction, adaptive functioning, and reduced caregiver stress in autistic preschoolers. Nonverbal development improvements were only observed in one-on-one interventions [4].

- **Exercise interventions for motor skills** (2026-09-18, *Meta-Analysis*)
  A dose-response meta-analysis of 46 exercise sessions (45 minutes each) demonstrated significant improvements in overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with autism. Effects varied by assessment timing, with exploratory peak effects noted at ~46 sessions [1].

- **Deep brain stimulation (DBS)** (2026 Dec, advance publication, *Case Reports*)
  A 12-month case study reported sustained reductions in core autism symptoms (e.g., ATEC, RBS-R scores) after bilateral dual-target ALIC-NAcc DBS in a child with severe autism. No adverse events were observed, though this was a single-case study [5].

## Key studies

1. **Wang et al. (2026)**, meta-analysis: “Exercise significantly improved overall, gross, and fine motor skills in children with ASD.” [1]
2. **Hao et al. (2026)**, systematic review: “There was a significant increase in XR autism research after 2020, with virtual reality (VR) as the largest modality (n = 61, 53.0%), followed by augmented reality (AR) (n = 36, 31.3%).” [2]
3. **Restoy et al. (2024)**, meta-analysis: “Studies of non-pharmacological interventions showed significant improvement in both ER and ED.” [7]
4. **Sandbank et al. (2023)**, meta-analysis: “When effects were then restricted to exclude those at high risk of detection bias, only one significant summary effect was estimated-naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions on measures of diagnostic characteristics of autism (0.30, 0.03 to 0.57; P=0.03).” [8]
5. **Iffland et al. (2023)**, systematic review: “Most studies involved children (53 studies involved only children under 13 years), children and adolescents (37 studies), adolescents only (2 studies) children and adults (16 studies), or adults only (23 studies).” [6]

## Conflicting evidence
The **Sandbank et al. (2023)** meta-analysis found significant benefits of behavioral and naturalistic developmental interventions for social-emotional outcomes and adaptive behavior in children with ASD, while **Anderson et al. (2023)** reported that autistic adults described traumatic experiences and long-term negative consequences from applied behavior analysis (ABA), calling it unethical. No direct comparison between these findings was retrieved, but the conflicting perspectives on ABA’s efficacy and ethical implications remain unresolved.

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy of extended reality (XR) interventions**: While preliminary evidence suggests benefits for social communication and engagement, no large-scale randomized controlled trials have yet established long-term clinical outcomes or optimal technical parameters (e.g., VR vs. AR) for children with ASD.
- **Feasibility of group-based naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions (NDBIs)**: Though Seng et al. (2026) demonstrated feasibility and acceptability, further research is needed to compare their effectiveness to one-on-one interventions across diverse populations and settings.
- **Dose-response relationships for pharmacologic interventions**: While atypical antipsychotics show short-term benefits for irritability [6], optimal dosing, long-term safety, and individual variability remain understudied, particularly in younger children.
- **Association between sleep spindle characteristics and ASD severity**: Erçelebi et al. (2026) identified potential neurophysiological markers, but their predictive value for intervention targeting or diagnostic stratification has not been supported in prospective trials.
- **Effectiveness of community-based adaptive autism interventions**: NCT04283045 (completed) reported results for social communicative utterances, but broader outcomes (e.g., long-term behavioral or cognitive gains) and scalability in real-world settings require further investigation.
- **Long-term outcomes of deep brain stimulation (DBS)**: Shi et al. (2026) showed sustained symptom reduction in a single case over 12 months, but no multicenter trials or comparative studies exist to assess safety, durability, or applicability to less severe ASD cases.

## Evidence limitations

- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses appear only where an FDA label gives them for this condition (section 2.1); for any other use, see a prescribing source. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
