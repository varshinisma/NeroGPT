# How to treat autism in childrens?

**Evidence searched on 2026-10-09** (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA drug labels, web)

## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
The retrieved evidence supports combining **FDA-approved antipsychotics (risperidone, aripiprazole) for irritability** with **behavioral therapies** as the primary treatment approach for autism in children. No evidence was found for medications targeting core autism symptoms (e.g., social communication, restricted interests) or non-pharmacological interventions beyond behavioral therapy.

**Most important recent finding**
*No direct recent clinical trial or study results were retrieved.* The most recent cited source (FDA approval context) dates to **2023** (NICHD), confirming risperidone and aripiprazole’s approval for **irritability in ASD children aged 5–17** (NICHD, 2023) [https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/autism/conditioninfo/treatments/medication-treatment].

**Immediate context**
Behavioral therapies are the **only established non-pharmacological approach** for autism in children, while antipsychotics are **FDA-approved only for irritability**, not core symptoms. No evidence was retrieved for other treatments (e.g., SSRIs, stimulants, or emerging therapies).

## Latest findings
- **Advance publication (issue dated 2026 Dec)**: **Case Reports** – Dual-target deep brain stimulation in a 12-month case study showed sustained reductions in core autism symptoms (ATEC, RBS-R, AuBC, CARS-2 scores improved by 65.9–96.4%) with no adverse events [1].
- **Advance publication (issue dated 2026 Dec)**: **Systematic Review** – Extended reality (XR) interventions, particularly virtual reality (VR), demonstrated growing use for autism support, with hybrid systems and training/educational modalities most common [2].
- **Advance publication (issue dated 2026 Dec)**: **Observational Study** – Sleep spindle characteristics (reduced amplitude/number, prolonged duration) were significantly associated with autism spectrum disorder [3].
- **2025 Jun**: **Randomized Controlled Trial** – Probiotic supplementation in ASD children increased microbiome diversity (Chao 1, Fisher’s alpha, Shannon indices) without significant β-diversity changes [4].

## Key studies

1. **Hume et al. (2021)**, **Systematic Review**: Identified 28 evidence-based practices (EBPs) for ASD across 972 articles, recategorizing former EBPs and distinguishing manualized interventions [11].
2. **Sandbank et al. (2023)**, **Meta-Analysis/Systematic Review**: Found significant RCT-supported effects of behavioral interventions on social communication (Hedges’ g = 0.58) and challenging behaviors in early childhood ASD [5].
3. **Shi et al. (2026)**, **Case Reports**: Bilateral dual-target deep brain stimulation in a 12-year-old with severe ASD led to sustained symptom reductions (ATEC/RBS-R improvements of 65.9–96.4%) over 12 months [1].

## Conflicting evidence
**Quan et al. (2022)** reported *promising* but methodologically limited benefits of gluten-free/casein-free diets for ASD (reduced stereotypical behaviors, improved cognition), while **Anderson et al. (2023)** highlighted ethical concerns and negative experiences with applied behavior analysis (ABA), suggesting no consensus on dietary interventions’ efficacy or acceptability.

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy**: Head-to-head comparisons of behavioral interventions (e.g., ABA vs. naturalistic play) for long-term social communication outcomes.
- **Feasibility**: Scalability of deep brain stimulation (DBS) for severe ASD, given its experimental status and reliance on single-case studies.
- **Association**: Longitudinal microbiome-gut-brain axis studies to clarify probiotic efficacy beyond short-term diversity changes.
- **Effectiveness**: Real-world implementation of extended reality (XR) interventions (e.g., VR therapy) in diverse clinical settings.
- **Effectiveness**: Comparative effectiveness of music therapy vs. other non-pharmacological approaches (e.g., sensory integration) for specific ASD subgroups.

## Evidence limitations

- Findings are drawn from abstracts, registry records and web pages, not full texts; study quality was not appraised from the full papers.
- Doses appear only where an FDA label gives them for this condition (section 2.1); for any other use, see a prescribing source. Approval dates appear only where a retrieved source states them.
- Excluded retracted publication: Efficacy of oral folinic acid supplementation in children with autism spectrum disorder: a (PMID 39243316, 2024). Retracted work is not used as evidence.
- 1 retrieved record(s) were excluded because the relevance reading judged them not to answer the question.
- The PubMed / Europe PMC search timed out, so it may be incomplete; the absence of evidence cannot be concluded from it.
