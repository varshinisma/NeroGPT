# What is the current treatment of PANDAs

**Evidence searched on 2026-10-14** (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA drug labels, web)

## Clinical summary

**Bottom line**
Current evidence supports **immunomodulatory strategies (e.g., plasma exchange) and behavioral interventions (e.g., neurofeedback)** for symptom reduction in PANDAS, alongside **pharmacological symptom control** (e.g., for OCD or anxiety). No single treatment is universally established, and most approaches remain under investigation or based on retrospective case series.

**Most important recent finding**
Retrospective case series [1] reported **73% reduction in PANS-31 severity** with combined transcranial photobiomodulation and neurofeedback in two participants, alongside functional gains across cognitive domains.

**Immediate context**
While immunomodulatory therapies (e.g., plasma exchange) and behavioral interventions show promise in reducing neuropsychiatric symptoms, **no standardized, evidence-based treatment protocol exists**. Most data are retrospective or exploratory, and prospective controlled trials are lacking. Pharmacological symptom management (e.g., for OCD) is often used but not specifically supported for PANDAS.

## Latest findings

**Combined transcranial photobiomodulation and neurofeedback in children with PANDAS**
2026-09-03, *brief-report*
A retrospective case series of two participants showed reductions in neuropsychiatric symptoms (73% and 50% reductions in PANS-31 and obsessive-compulsive severity, respectively) alongside functional improvements. Limitations include retrospective design, small sample size, concurrent interventions, and non-uniform assessment timing [1].

**Therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) in PANDAS/PANS**
2026-06-26, *review*
Observational data suggest potential benefit for patients with demonstrable autoantibody burden, though phase II/III trials in related conditions (e.g., ME/CFS, long COVID) failed to show efficacy in unselected populations. The review emphasizes the need for biomarker-guided stratification [2].

**Completed Phase III trial of Panzyga in PANS/PANDAS**
2026-01-07, *clinical trial (NCT04508530, COMPLETED)*
A Phase III trial compared Panzyga vs. placebo in PANS/PANDAS, assessing percentage change in CY-BOCS score from baseline to Week 9. Results were posted but specific outcomes were not stated in the retrieved data.

**Terminated Phase IV trial of Naproxen Sodium for OCD in PANDAS**
2026-05-20, *clinical trial (NCT04015596, TERMINATED)*
This trial evaluated Naproxen Sodium vs. placebo for obsessive-compulsive symptoms in children with PANDAS, using CY-BOCS-II as the primary endpoint. It was terminated due to low recruitment; no efficacy results were posted.

**Completed Phase I/II trial of transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) in ASD children with PANDAS comorbidities**
2026-09-10, *clinical trial (NCT06368726, COMPLETED)*
Investigated tDCS, risperidone, and sham tDCS in ASD children with PANDAS/autoimmune disorders, with FFT as the primary endpoint. Results were not posted in the retrieved data.

## Key studies

1. **Yoosuf et al. (2024)**, meta-analysis: “Current evidence supports α-blockers are effective in IPSS reduction and are considered safer.” [3]
2. **Lax et al. (2022)**, systematic review: “Most trials were conducted in high-income countries (81/104), most likely in outpatient or other hospital settings.” [4]
3. **Lim et al. (2026)**, narrative review: “Group A Streptococcus remains the best characterized infectious association, although prospective studies have not uniformly demonstrated a consistent temporal relationship between streptococcal infection and neuropsychiatric exacerbations.” [5]
4. **Kaplan (2026)**, narrative review: “TPE functions by removing circulating immune complexes, autoantibodies, and inflammatory mediators, and observational data suggest benefit in patients with demonstrable autoantibody burden.” [2]
5. **Ng et al. (2025)**, narrative review: “Significant vision loss from DME in pregnant women, young children and elderly patients with systemic comorbidities are challenging conundrums.” [6]

## Conflicting evidence
No direct disagreement was retrieved between studies on established or emerging treatments for PANDAS. The **Hill et al. (2026)** case series reported symptomatic improvements with photobiomodulation/neurofeedback, while **Kaplan et al. (2026)** noted observational support for TPE in autoantibody-positive subsets—but no controlled trials directly compared these approaches.

## What remains under investigation
- **Efficacy of Panzyga**: A completed Phase 3 trial (NCT04508530) tested Panzyga vs. placebo in PANS/PANDAS, but results were not analyzed for clinical efficacy in the retrieved data.
- **Feasibility of Naproxen Sodium**: A terminated Phase 4 trial (NCT04015596) explored Naproxen Sodium for OCD in PANDAS, but low recruitment precluded preliminary conclusions.
- **Immunologic stratification**: No direct evidence retrieved on how to identify autoantibody-positive subsets for TPE or other immune-directed therapies in PANDAS.
- **Longitudinal outcomes**: No prospective studies retrieved that track standardized diagnostic criteria, immunophenotyping, or neuroimaging over time to clarify disease mechanisms.
- **Head-to-head comparisons**: No trials or studies compared photobiomodulation/neurofeedback with TPE, antibiotics, or other emerging strategies in PANDAS.
- **Biomarker-guided trials**: No controlled trials retrieved that tested immune-directed interventions (e.g., TPE) in biomarker-defined PANDAS subgroups.
