# What is the latest evidence on tolebrutinib in multiple sclerosis?

**Bottom line** – Tolebrutinib reduced relapse activity in relapsing‑remitting MS and slowed disability progression in non‑relapsing secondary‑progressive MS in two pivotal phase‑III NEJM trials published in 2025, but it remains **unapproved in the United States (FDA complete‑response letter, Dec 2025)** while receiving **EU marketing authorization (2026)**; safety data are reassuring but liver‑related adverse events continue to be monitored (2026 safety review)【1†PMID 40202623】【2†PMID 40202696】【3†PMID 42546561】【8†url】.  

**Evidence searched on 2026‑10‑06**  

**Most recent key finding** – In a phase‑III trial of participants with non‑relapsing secondary‑progressive MS, tolebrutinib significantly reduced the proportion of patients with 6‑month confirmed disability progression versus placebo (primary endpoint met) (May 15 2025)【2†PMID 40202696】.  

**Immediate context** – BTK inhibition is an emerging therapeutic strategy for both inflammatory and neurodegenerative aspects of MS. Tolebrutinib is the first brain‑penetrant BTK inhibitor to demonstrate efficacy across MS phenotypes, prompting EU approval (Cenrifki) in 2026, while the FDA has so far issued a complete‑response letter, delaying US approval【8†url】【9†url】. Ongoing long‑term safety studies continue to assess hepatic and other adverse events【3†PMID 42546561】.

### Current evidence hierarchy
- **Established DMTs** (ocrelizumab, siponimod, cladribine) – long‑term efficacy & safety data.  
- **Emerging/Approved BTK inhibitor** – Tolebrutinib (EU, 2026).  
- **Investigational BTK inhibitors** – fenebrutinib (FDA NDA under priority review, 2026) – not directly relevant to tolebrutinib but indicates class activity.
